# BUDCO Non-Negotiables

**Status:** Permanent — technology-independent  
**Working name:** BUDCO

These principles are intentionally technology-independent.

They must remain valid whether BUDCO is delivered as Android, Desktop, Web, Cloud, AI-assisted, self-hosted, enterprise, community — or any future form the product takes.

Architecture may evolve. Stacks may change. Contributors may come and go.  
**These non-negotiables must survive every rewrite.**

Long-term vision: [VISION_2030.md](VISION_2030.md)  
Engineering rules (current Android companion): [PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md](PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md)

---

## 1. Mission

BUDCO exists to strengthen businesses through technology.

Our success is measured by the capability we create, not the dependency we create.

---

## 2. Inclusion

Every legitimate business is welcome.

No business is excluded because of:

- size
- industry
- revenue
- geography
- maturity

Examples include — and are **not limits**:

- solo entrepreneur
- home business
- kirana
- bakery
- tailor
- trader
- manufacturer
- reseller
- consultant
- NGO
- educational institution
- technology company
- research organization
- aerospace company
- multinational enterprise

The platform must scale naturally across all of them.

---

## 3. Technology

Technology should scale down as well as scale up.

Enterprise capability should not require enterprise complexity.

---

## 4. User Experience

Reduce:

- clicks
- typing
- waiting
- confusion
- repetition

Increase:

- confidence
- speed
- trust
- clarity

---

## 5. AI

AI should:

- assist — not control
- recommend — not surprise
- reduce work — not create work

---

## 6. Business Philosophy

We do not build against competitors.

We build for users.

We strengthen businesses.

We respect existing ecosystems.

We integrate whenever possible.

---

## 7. Engineering

Architecture may evolve.

Languages may change.

Frameworks may change.

Platforms may change.

**Mission does not.**

---

## 8. Final Principle

Every significant feature should answer:

> Does this genuinely make someone's business stronger?

If not, reconsider it.
